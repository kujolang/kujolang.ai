# Comparison editorial policy

Reviewed 2026-10-19. Kujo publishes these comparisons from its own perspective.

1. Compare the actual project and pinned version; verify canonical identity before attributing repository capabilities.
2. Prefer executable implementations and tests, then current documentation, releases, maintained examples, specifications and marketing.
3. State whether evidence was executed, inspected or only documented. An inspected test is not a passed test.
4. Distinguish language, first-party ecosystem, third-party integration and experimental features. Never turn a receipt into a proof of semantic correctness.
5. Use supported, unsupported, partial, not applicable and unknown deliberately. Missing documentation is unknown, not unsupported.
6. Credit competitor strengths and disclose Kujo limitations. Recommendations are editorial judgments, not objective rankings.
7. Pin examples and test when practical. Publish benchmark claims only with comparable workloads, commands, environment, outcomes, repetitions and limitations.
8. Keep HTML and Markdown generated from the same catalog; validate source references, dates and related links before merging.
9. Record a new verification date only after actual review. Recheck on relevant releases and at least quarterly; do not auto-refresh dates.
10. Never invent search volumes, metrics, citations, testimonials or AI recommendations. No hidden content or crawler instructions.

Report corrections at https://github.com/kujolang/kujolang.ai/issues/new with the page, version and primary source. Source identity gaps must remain visible until resolved. Production publication requires the site owner's authorization.
